TETRAform Autofill Extension vs Chrome Autofill: Multiple Profiles, Custom Fields, Stored Locally

By the TETRAform team · Published November 20, 2026

Chrome already fills forms for you. It remembers your addresses, payment cards and passwords, and it is a good choice for checkout pages and sign-ins. So why install a form filler Chrome extension at all?

Because a lot of forms ask for things Chrome doesn't keep: your LinkedIn URL, notice period, salary expectations, student ID, VAT number, or a short answer you have typed twenty times. This guide compares Chrome autofill with TETRAform, a free autofill Chrome extension, and shows where each one fits.

The short answer

  • Use Chrome autofill for addresses, payment cards and passwords. It is built in and works well on checkout and sign-in pages.
  • Add TETRAform when you fill the same longer forms again and again: job applications, scholarship or school forms, client and supplier forms. You save whole profiles, with any fields you choose, and fill them in one click.
  • The two work side by side. TETRAform never saves passwords or card details, so Chrome keeps that job.

What Chrome autofill does well

According to Google's Chrome help page, Chrome can fill names, passwords, addresses and payment details, and it groups saved information into passwords, payments, contact info, identity documents and travel. You can save several addresses and pick one from the dropdown. If you are signed in to Chrome, your saved details also appear on your other signed-in devices.

For everyday shopping and logins, that is often all you need.

Where Chrome autofill stops

Chrome fills the types of information it knows about. Many real forms go further:

  • Job applications: LinkedIn and portfolio links, current job title, years of experience, notice period, salary expectations, work authorisation.
  • School and scholarship forms: student ID, university, degree, graduation date, short personal answers.
  • Business forms: company name, VAT or tax number, IBAN, website, a standard description of your services.

Chrome also doesn't let you choose which details belong together. Your work email, your personal email and your job-hunting details all sit in the same place, and you pick each field from a dropdown.

What TETRAform adds

Named profiles that cover the whole form

Create a profile for each part of your life, for example Job Applications, Student, Freelance or Personal. Each profile holds its own set of fields, not just an address. You can even keep a profile in a second language for forms in that language. Pick a profile, press Fill, and TETRAform fills every field it recognises from that profile.

You choose what to save

Fill in a form once, press Save, and TETRAform saves the fields from that page into your current profile. You decide which forms to save from, and you can rename, move or delete any field afterwards. Chrome, by contrast, offers to save the types of information it supports.

Custom fields and groups

Add any field you need, such as "Notice period" or "Why do you want this job?", and organise fields into groups like Contact, Address, Work, Education or your own. Move a field to another group whenever you like.

The right profile on each site

Link a profile to a website and TETRAform picks it automatically there: your job-search profile on a careers site, your business profile on a supplier portal. You can also turn TETRAform off completely on sites where you don't want it.

Stored on your device, no account

Your TETRAform profiles are saved in Chrome storage on your device and are never uploaded to TETRAform. There is no account to create. If you want to move to another computer, export your profiles to a JSON file and import it there. Keep that file safe, because it contains your saved details. The privacy policy explains exactly what is stored and the one case where data leaves your device (the optional feedback form).

This is a different approach from Chrome's newer AI features. In October 2026 Google announced that Chrome's enhanced autofill can suggest details from your Gmail, such as order numbers or flight details, using Gemini. It is rolling out in the US for adults who opt in. Some people will love that. Others prefer their form data to stay in their own browser.

Sensitive fields are skipped

TETRAform detects and skips passwords, card numbers, CVV codes, CAPTCHAs and one-time codes. Websites don't always label fields clearly, so review what you save and what gets filled.

When Chrome autofill is the better fit

  • You mostly fill checkout and sign-in forms.
  • You want your details on every device through your Google Account.
  • You don't want to install an extension.

What about Chrome profiles?

Some people create separate Chrome profiles (for example Work and Personal) to keep different autofill details apart. That works, but each Chrome profile is a whole separate browser, with its own bookmarks, history, tabs and logins. TETRAform profiles only hold form details, so you can switch from your job-search profile to your personal one on the same page, in the same window.

How to get started with TETRAform

  1. Add TETRAform to Chrome from the Chrome Web Store.
  2. Create a profile, for example Job Applications.
  3. Fill in a form you use often, then press Save in the Assistant Bar (or Ctrl+Shift+S).
  4. Next time, open a similar form and press Fill (or Ctrl+Shift+F). Check the form, then submit it.
